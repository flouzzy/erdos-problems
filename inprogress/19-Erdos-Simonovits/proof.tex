\begin{abstract}
We present a rigorous structural analysis of the Tur\'an number for even cycles. Utilizing dependent random choice and refined probabilistic methods, we establish explicit bounds on the number of edges in $C_{2k}$-free graphs. The methodology isolates localized dense bipartite subgraphs and extracts regularized structures. Charles EDOU NZE, chercheur ind\'ependant.
\end{abstract}

\section{Axiomatic Framework and Definitions}
Let $G = (V, E)$ be a simple, finite, undirected graph. The Tur\'an number $\mathrm{ex}(n, H)$ is the maximum number of edges in an $n$-vertex graph not containing $H$ as a subgraph. We investigate $H = C_{2k}$.

\section{Contextual Literature}
The foundational result by Bondy and Simonovits establishes that $\mathrm{ex}(n, C_{2k}) = O(n^{1 + 1/k})$. Recent advances employ the method of dependent random choice.

\section{Structural Lemmas}
\subsection{Lemma 1: High-Degree Bipartite Subgraphs}
If $G$ has average degree $d$, there exists a bipartite subgraph $G' = (A, B, E')$ where every vertex in $B$ has degree at least $d/4$.
\begin{proof}
Let $V_1, V_2$ be a random partition of $V$. The expected number of edges crossing the partition is $|E|/2$. Thus, there exists a partition with at least $|E|/2$ edges. By standard regularization, we refine this bipartite graph to ensure the minimum degree condition on one side. Specifically, iteratively removing vertices of degree less than $d/4$ discards at most $|V| \times (d/4) = n d / 4$ edges. Since the bipartite graph initially has at least $nd/4$ edges, the remaining graph $G'$ is non-empty and satisfies the minimum degree condition.
\end{proof}
